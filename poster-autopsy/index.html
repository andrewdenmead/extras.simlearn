Poster Autopsy

Fifteen film posters. Read the design before you read the film.

A film poster has about three seconds to tell you what kind of film it is, who it's for and how it will make you feel. Designers do it with a small set of tools, and with genre codes: looks that audiences have learned to recognise.

Here are fifteen posters from 1949 to 2014, sorted into five families. It doesn't matter whether you know the films. Read only the design.

Step 1Warm up

TypographyFont style, weight, size and placement. What does the lettering feel like?
ColourThe palette, the contrast, one accent colour. What mood?
CompositionWhere things sit, how big they are, where the lines lead your eye.
Imagery stylePainting, photo, silhouette, collage? Realistic or stylised?
WordsTagline, quotes, names, dates. What do they add to the picture?

Visual hierarchy: the order your eye reads a poster in: first, second, third. On every poster below you'll say what you noticed first.

  • The first thing you notice is …, and then your eye moves to …
  • The warm / cool / limited palette creates a … mood.
  • The heavy, condensed / thin, elegant / hand-drawn lettering feels …
  • By placing … in the centre / at the bottom / inside …, the designer suggests …
  • This poster follows / bends the genre code by …

Think of a film poster you remember: on a wall, at a bus stop, as a phone wallpaper. What do you remember about it: the image, the colours, the words? Why did it stick?
