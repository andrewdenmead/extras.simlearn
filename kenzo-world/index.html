KENZO World

A perfume ad that breaks the rules: a shot-by-shot reading

Spike Jonze's 2016 film for the perfume KENZO World, starring Margaret Qualley, starts like almost every perfume ad you've seen. Then it does something very different. Your job is to work out what changes, how the film shows it, and what it wants us to feel about the woman at its centre.

Step 2Ten shots, five tools

Mise-en-scène means everything placed in the frame. Each shot below has one focus. Write your own reading first; then open the model reading and compare.

SettingWhere are we, and what does the place expect of people?
Dress codeWhat does the costume say about status, era or role?
PropsWhich objects are there on purpose, and what do they stand for?
Lighting & colourHow is it lit, what palette, what mood?
Non-verbalWhat are her face, gestures and body saying?

Iconography: images a genre trains us to recognise. Perfume ads have their own: evening gowns, chandeliers, soft music, a beautiful woman being admired. Watch how this film uses those images, and then turns them upside down.

Step 3The story arc

Here's where you placed each shot. The film tells one of the oldest stories there is: someone stuck in a role, an escape, a testing of the rules, a gain in power, a breakthrough. Does your map show a clear line? Where were you unsure?

Where else have you seen this story shape: in films, myths, fairy tales or other ads? Why do you think it works so well for selling something?

Step 4Step back

Afterlife

In 2018, Taylor Swift's music video for "Delicate", directed by Joseph Kahn, was accused online of copying KENZO World: a woman bored at a glamorous event becomes invisible and lets loose with silly faces and wild dancing. Why might this ad have become a template that others copy? Is being copied a compliment, a problem, or both?

Step 5Write it up

In 120–150 words: how does KENZO World present a young woman breaking away from traditional expectations? Refer to at least two shots and use at least three of the five tools.

  • Although the film opens like a conventional perfume ad, …
  • In the first shot, she is positioned … , which suggests …
  • This contrasts sharply with … , where …
  • By the final shot, … . The film links the perfume with …
