Crisis communication

When something goes wrong, every word a company says, and every word it doesn't, gets noticed. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. When Deutsche Bahn announces a delay, which kind of announcement annoys you most? What would you want them to say instead?
  2. Why do false stories spread faster online than corrections? What, if anything, can be done about it?
  3. Would you rather work in a team that shares every problem openly, or one where managers hear bad news first? What are the effects of each?
  4. Online pile-ons can wreck a person's or a company's reputation in hours. Who has the power to stop one, and should they use it?

Docket 2

  1. Have you ever had your card refused, an app crash or your phone network go down at the worst moment? What did you do?
  2. What's the difference between keeping something private and keeping a secret? Give an example from work or university.
  3. Would you rather receive bad news by text, by email or in a phone call? Does it change if the news comes from a company or from your boss?
  4. Big companies employ PR teams to manage their image. Does that make them more honest, or just better at hiding things? What would a PR professional say?

Docket 3

  1. What's the worst customer-service response you've ever received? What made it so bad?
  2. Why do people often trust a random video from a stranger more than an official statement from a company?
  3. Would you rather a café you love closed for a week to fix a problem properly, or stayed open and fixed it while serving? What would the regulars say?
  4. Should companies be allowed to delete negative comments on their own social media pages? Who would be helped and who harmed?

Docket 4

  1. When did you last complain to a company? How long did it take to reply, and what did the reply say?
  2. Why do stories about hacking or data leaks frighten people more than other technical failures? What does that say about our trust in technology?
  3. Would you rather your company had a detailed crisis plan nobody reads, or an experienced team that improvises well? Why?
  4. When large companies make mistakes, small businesses often suffer most. Who should protect them: the company, insurers or the government?

Docket 5

  1. Have you ever stayed quiet in a discussion because you weren't sure of your facts? Looking back, was that the right call?
  2. What makes a message in a crisis sound human rather than robotic? Think of one from a company, university or landlord that got it right or wrong.
  3. Would you rather a company fixed a problem slowly but perfectly, or quickly with a risk of new mistakes? Who would prefer each?
  4. Social media means anyone can broadcast a crisis before the company even knows about it. Has that made companies more accountable, or just more panicky?

Docket 6

  1. Think of a time a group chat went wrong because someone misread a message. How was it sorted out?
  2. Is it ever right for a company to keep information from its own employees during a crisis? When, and why?
  3. Would you rather work in PR for a company in crisis, or as a journalist covering it? Which job would be harder?
  4. Some countries have laws that punish spreading false information. Is that a good way to protect people, or a danger to free speech? What would each side say?

Docket 7

  1. Which company or public figure do you think handled a crisis really well, or really badly? What did they do?
  2. Why do some companies recover from a scandal stronger than before, while others never recover?
  3. Would you rather stay with a company that once let you down but handled it well, or switch to one that's never failed but you know little about? Why?
  4. When a crisis happens in one country, should a global company respond the same way everywhere, or adapt to each country's culture? What would local managers say?

Docket 8

  1. Have you ever posted something online and wished you could delete it? What did you learn?
  2. Why do companies use phrases like 'we take this very seriously'? Do they work on you, and why or why not?
  3. Would you rather your favourite restaurant answered a bad review with a funny reply, a serious reply or no reply at all? Why?
  4. Customers hit by a company's mistake often get an apology but no money. Should apologies always come with compensation? Who would pay in the end?
