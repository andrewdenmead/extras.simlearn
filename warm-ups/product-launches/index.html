Warm-up · EBC · C1

Product launches

Thousands of new products are launched every year, and most of them disappear quickly. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk about why some launches work, and what companies risk when they hurry or hold back.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Think of a product you bought in its first week on sale. What made you want it early, and was it worth it?
  2. Which annoys you more as a customer: a product that sells out at launch, or one that arrives full of small problems?
  3. Your team can launch now with one known weakness or wait three months to fix it. Which would you choose, and who would disagree?
  4. When a launch goes wrong, who should carry the blame: the people who pushed for speed, or the people who saw problems and stayed quiet?

Docket 2

  1. Which new product or service have you seen launched in Berlin recently? What did the company do to get people talking?
  2. The Deutschlandticket launched at €49 and has become more expensive since. Is it fair to win customers with a launch price and raise it later? Who gains?
  3. You are launching a new drink. Would you rather be first on the shelf, or second with a better product? Why?
  4. Launches are often timed to a big customer's or an event's deadline. When should a company refuse an outside deadline, and what does refusing cost?

Docket 3

  1. Have you ever been excited by an advert for a new product and then disappointed by the real thing? What went wrong?
  2. Which matters more at a launch: what the product actually does, or the story that is told about it? Give an example.
  3. Marketing wants half the launch budget for a big event; the product team wants more testing. Who gets the money, and how do you tell the other side?
  4. Should companies be allowed to call a product 'new and improved' when only the packaging has changed? Where would you draw the line?

Docket 4

  1. What is the last product you tried simply because it was new? Did you buy it a second time, and why?
  2. Lidl and Aldi sell some products for a few weeks and then remove them. Is that clever marketing or just frustrating? Explain from both sides.
  3. Would you rather work for a company that launches many products and drops most, or one that launches rarely but carefully? Why?
  4. Who should decide when a product is ready to launch: the people who sell it, or the people who make it? What does each side miss?

Docket 5

  1. Which product launch do you remember from the news or social media, good or bad? Why has it stuck in your memory?
  2. Do waiting lists and 'limited first editions' make you want a product more, or put you off? Give an example.
  3. Your student start-up has one big shop ready to stock you, but it wants changes to your product. How far would you go, and where would you stop?
  4. Sustainability is now a selling point at almost every launch. Is it a company's job to change shoppers' habits, or just to follow them?

Docket 6

  1. Have you ever queued, pre-ordered or stayed up late for a launch? What would make you do it again?
  2. Is it better for a new product to be loved by a few passionate people or noticed a little by everyone? Who benefits from each?
  3. Your best engineer says the product isn't ready; your biggest customer wants it next month. Whose side do you take, and how do you tell the other person?
  4. How should companies treat staff whose bold launch failed: reward the courage, or hold them responsible? What message does each choice send?

Docket 7

  1. Which everyday product do you think is overdue for a new version? What would you change first, and why?
  2. Would you pay more for a product just because it is the 'eco' version? How much more, and what proof would you want?
  3. A competitor is about to launch something similar to yours. Do you rush to beat them, or let them go first and learn from their mistakes?
  4. When a new product turns out worse than promised, who is responsible: the company, the shops that sold it, or the shoppers who believed the hype?

Docket 8

  1. What new drink or snack have you noticed in a Späti or supermarket recently? What made you notice it, or ignore it?
  2. Which can recover more easily: a launch with huge hype and a disappointing product, or a quiet launch with a great product? Why?
  3. Your team has six weeks until launch and something must go. Would you cut testing, marketing or extra features? Defend your choice.
  4. Should governments push companies to launch greener products, for example through packaging taxes, or should shoppers decide? Who would gain and lose?
