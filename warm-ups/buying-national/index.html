Buying national

More and more governments want their own country to make the things it needs. Pick a docket, talk it through with your neighbour, and give reasons for your views.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. When you see 'Made in Germany' on a product, how much extra, if anything, would you pay for it? Does it depend on the product?
  2. Why do people often trust products from their own country more, even when they can't say where the parts came from?
  3. A cheaper bike made in Asia or a dearer one made in Brandenburg: which would you buy, and what would change your mind?
  4. If making things at home keeps a country safer in a crisis but poorer in normal times, is that a price worth paying? Who would disagree?

Docket 2

  1. Look at the clothes you're wearing. Do you know where any of them were made, and how much does it matter to you?
  2. During the pandemic, Germany ran short of masks and medicines. What should a country always be able to make for itself, and why?
  3. Keep a struggling steelworks open with public money, or let it close and retrain the workers? Which would you choose, and why?
  4. When governments 'buy national', is that patriotism, protectionism or common sense? How would a trading partner see it?

Docket 3

  1. Do you pick regional food at the supermarket, or the cheapest option? What makes you switch?
  2. Is 'buy local' mainly about the environment, about jobs or about identity? Which matters most to the people you know?
  3. A factory with well-paid jobs that pollutes a little, or a clean office park with fewer, lower-paid jobs: which would you want in your town?
  4. If every country bought mostly from itself, would the world be more secure or more hostile? What would someone who disagrees say?

Docket 4

  1. What product from your home region or country would you defend against cheaper imports? Why that one?
  2. Scotland sells whisky and salmon all over the world. How can a country that relies on exports also ask its own people to buy national?
  3. Your university canteen can buy cheaper imported food or dearer food from Brandenburg farms. Which should it choose, and who should decide?
  4. Free trade has made many countries richer overall but hollowed out particular towns. Can a policy count as a success if some places lose badly?

Docket 5

  1. Have you ever paid more on purpose to support a local business, like a Späti, a bakery or a bookshop? What made it worth it?
  2. Why do you think 'bring the jobs home' has become such a popular promise in so many countries?
  3. Cheap solar panels from abroad now, or dearer ones made at home later: which would help the climate more, and why?
  4. Should rich countries charge for the carbon in imported goods, even if poorer exporting countries call it unfair? What would those countries argue?

Docket 6

  1. Which everyday products would you struggle to find if imports stopped tomorrow? What would you miss first?
  2. When is it fair for a government to give a contract to a local firm, even though a foreign firm offered a better deal?
  3. Spend public money on a factory in a struggling former mining town, or in a booming city where it would grow fastest? Why?
  4. Do 'buy national' rules mainly protect workers, or the companies that win the contracts? What would a union say, and a business?

Docket 7

  1. Has a shortage ever affected you: empty shelves, delayed parts, a medicine you couldn't get? What did it teach you?
  2. How far should a country go to avoid depending on one other country for vital goods, like computer chips or medicines?
  3. A German car with parts from twenty countries, or a Chinese car built almost entirely in China: which is more 'foreign', and why?
  4. Is it hypocritical for countries that preached free trade for decades to protect their own industries now? What would their defenders say?

Docket 8

  1. Where was the last thing you bought online actually made? How easy was it to find out?
  2. 'Made in China' once meant cheap; now it often means high-tech. What changed, and what does that mean for Europe?
  3. A job for your neighbour at a local factory, or slightly lower prices for everyone in the country: if you had to choose, which?
  4. When one country protects its industries, others often hit back. Is that a reason never to start, or a risk worth taking for security?
