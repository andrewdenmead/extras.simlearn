Design and sustainability

Greener design sounds simple until it costs more, looks different or asks people to change. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. What's one sustainable habit you've kept and one you've given up? What made the difference?
  2. Fast fashion is cheap, fun and bad for the planet. Is it fair to criticise people who can't afford anything else?
  3. A weekend from Berlin to Munich: a cheap flight or a slower, more expensive train? Be honest, and explain your choice.
  4. Should designers be judged on how sustainable their work is, the way architects are judged on safety? What would critics say?

Docket 2

  1. Do you buy second-hand clothes, furniture or phones? What stops you buying more?
  2. Who has the most power to make design more sustainable: consumers, companies or governments? Why?
  3. Would you rather design an exciting product that sells a million, or a less exciting one with half the environmental impact? Why?
  4. Many sustainable products cost more. Is sustainable design becoming a luxury for people who can afford it? What could designers do about it?

Docket 3

  1. How do you usually get around Berlin: bike, BVG, car, scooter? What would make you change?
  2. Streaming and cloud storage use a lot of energy in data centres. Should app and web designers worry about that, or is it too small to matter?
  3. You're organising a festival: ban single-use cups and charge everyone a deposit, or keep things easy and cheap? Which would you choose?
  4. An online shop could show each delivery's CO2 next to the 'Buy' button. Would that change what people order, or just annoy them? Who would object?

Docket 4

  1. Have you ever felt guilty about something you bought? What made you feel that way?
  2. Can a product really be sustainable if its main purpose is fashion and fun? Think of trainers or phone cases.
  3. A car-free centre for Berlin would make life harder for some workers and older residents. Would you choose it? Who wins, who loses?
  4. Some cities have banned adverts for high-carbon products like SUVs or cheap flights. Is that fair? What would someone who disagrees say?

Docket 5

  1. What design change, in a shop, a product or the city, has made it easier for you to live more sustainably?
  2. Some say individual choices hardly matter when big companies cause most emissions. Do you agree? Why?
  3. A festival stage: built new for one weekend, or from reused parts that look less impressive? Which would you design, and who would complain?
  4. Should design schools teach sustainability in every course, or would that limit creative freedom? What would a student who disagrees say?

Docket 6

  1. Which sustainable product or brand do you actually enjoy using, not just admire? What makes it work?
  2. Is a product made locally always more sustainable than one shipped from far away? What else matters?
  3. A smaller flat close to the centre, or a bigger one outside with a long commute: which would you choose, and which is more sustainable?
  4. If a sustainable design makes a company less profitable, how long should the company stick with it? Who should decide?

Docket 7

  1. How do you feel when you see perfectly usable furniture dumped on a Berlin pavement? What should happen to it?
  2. Electric cars have no exhaust, but making their batteries has a big environmental cost. How would you compare them with petrol cars?
  3. A three-day class trip: Scotland by plane, or the Baltic coast by train? Which would you choose, and what would the class say?
  4. Is it ever right for a government to ban a popular product, like plastic straws, for environmental reasons? Who should decide?

Docket 8

  1. Do your friends or family talk much about sustainability? Does it ever lead to arguments? About what?
  2. Some say the most sustainable product is the one you never buy. Is that a serious idea for designers, or just a clever slogan?
  3. Your studio is offered a big contract designing free promotional gifts for a trade fair. Would you take it, change the brief or refuse? Why?
  4. Do young people carry an unfair share of the pressure to live sustainably? What would an older person say in reply?
