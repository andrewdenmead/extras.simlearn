Warm-up · EIM · C1/C2

Old giants, new rivals

Big, established companies and fast new challengers: who wins, who adapts, and why? Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which brand or product from your childhood has now disappeared, or nearly? Why do you think it failed?
  2. Some 'revolutionary' technologies fizzle out, and the companies that waited look wise. Think of an example. How can a company tell hype from a real threat?
  3. Would you rather work for a big, established company with stable jobs, or a small challenger trying to disrupt it? Why?
  4. When a famous company collapses, thousands lose their jobs. Should governments rescue giants, or let new companies take their place? What would someone who disagrees say?

Docket 2

  1. Which app or service have you switched to recently because it was simply easier? What would make you switch back?
  2. What do Nokia, Blockbuster and Myspace have in common? Which famous company today could be next?
  3. Would you rather your phone lasted ten years with few new features, or were replaced every two years with the latest ones? What does your answer say about customers?
  4. German carmakers became world leaders with petrol and diesel engines. How well are they handling electric cars, and what's holding them back?

Docket 3

  1. Which habit of your parents' generation, like paper maps, CDs or landlines, do you think you'll never have? What replaced it?
  2. Why do people inside big companies often resist new ideas, even good ones? What would you do as a new employee with a big idea?
  3. Would you rather run a giant company that's slowly declining, or a tiny start-up that might grow or fail? Why?
  4. Some say disruption is healthy: old giants fall and better companies replace them. Others say it destroys jobs, skills and communities. Which view convinces you more?

Docket 4

  1. What's one technology you resisted at first but now couldn't live without? What changed your mind?
  2. Why do people stay loyal to companies like Deutsche Telekom, the Sparkasse or the BBC even when cheaper alternatives exist?
  3. If you ran a company, how would you split money between what earns profits today and ideas that might pay off in ten years? Defend your split.
  4. Many giants were brought down not by competitors but by changing customer habits. Which habit change today could hurt a big company you know?

Docket 5

  1. Which social media platform did you use most at fourteen? Why did you leave it, or why didn't you?
  2. Why do customers sometimes stay with old, slow companies for years, then suddenly all leave at once?
  3. Would you rather use a product that does one thing perfectly, or one that does many things reasonably well? Which kind of company is more likely to survive?
  4. Can a company be too big to change? What would you say to someone who thinks size is always an advantage?

Docket 6

  1. Think of a family business or local shop that has adapted well to the internet. What did it do right?
  2. Older companies are often mocked as slow, yet many outlast the start-ups that challenged them. When is moving slowly the smarter strategy?
  3. Would you rather be a customer of a company that changes fast and sometimes gets it wrong, or one that changes slowly and rarely makes mistakes? Why?
  4. Apps shook up taxis, hotels and travel agents. Was that progress for everyone, or did someone pay the price? Who?

Docket 7

  1. Think of a product you were sure would be a hit but flopped, or one you laughed at that succeeded. What did you miss?
  2. In some markets start-ups beat giants; in others the giants win easily. What gives each side its advantage?
  3. Would you rather join a company at its peak or during a crisis? What could you learn in each?
  4. Should society protect traditional industries like coal, cars or newspapers because of the communities around them, or let them shrink? What would people in those communities say?

Docket 8

  1. Which famous brand do you think your children will never have heard of? Why?
  2. 'If it isn't broken, don't fix it.' When is that good advice in business, and when is it dangerous?
  3. Which matters most for a company's long-term survival: a great product, loyal customers or a willingness to change? Rank them and defend your order.
  4. When a big company fails, should its leaders be blamed, or were they simply unlucky with timing? Think of a real example.
