Warm-up · EBC · C1

Brand identity

A brand is more than a logo: it is a personality, a story and a promise. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk about what makes a brand feel like itself, and what happens to that feeling as a company grows.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which brand would you describe as 'you'? What does it say about you that another brand wouldn't?
  2. When a small brand you love appears in every supermarket, does it feel more successful or less special? Why?
  3. You design the logo for a friend's small business. They want it to look 'professional'; you want to keep its handmade charm. How would you settle it?
  4. Should brands take a public position on political or social issues, or stay out of them? What do they risk either way?

Docket 2

  1. Think of a product you would recognise without seeing its name. What makes it so recognisable?
  2. Which matters more to a brand's identity: what the product is, or the story of who makes it? Give an example.
  3. Your café becomes popular and a chain starts copying its style. Would you change your look to stay different, or keep it and compete? Why?
  4. How far do customers have a right to feel betrayed when a brand they love changes? Or is a brand simply the company's property?

Docket 3

  1. Have you ever stopped buying a brand because it changed its packaging, recipe or style? What exactly put you off?
  2. Is a brand's 'personality' real, or just something marketing teams invent? Give an example either way.
  3. Your brand's best-known slogan has become an internet joke. Would you keep it, drop it, or join in with the joke? Why?
  4. As brands grow, many seem to lose what made them special. Is that unavoidable, or a choice companies make? Give an example.

Docket 4

  1. What do your clothes, bag or phone case say about you? Is that a message you chose?
  2. How much more do you trust a product when you know who the people behind it are? Why?
  3. A supermarket will stock your small brand but wants your logo smaller and its own 'local hero' sticker on top. What would you do?
  4. Can a brand stay 'authentic' as it grows, or is authenticity something it can only lose? What would a marketer say?

Docket 5

  1. Which brand from your home town or region are you proud of? What would make you stop being proud of it?
  2. Fritz-Kola started in Hamburg with two friends and a few crates. How can a brand still feel 'underground' once it sells millions of bottles?
  3. Your brand is famous for one colour. A designer says it looks cheap on supermarket shelves. Would you change it, test it, or ignore her?
  4. Who really owns a brand's identity: the founders, the staff, the customers or the investors? Who should have the final word?

Docket 6

  1. Describe a shop, bar or Späti whose atmosphere you love. Could it keep that atmosphere if there were fifty of them?
  2. Which do you value more in a brand: consistency, so it's always the same, or surprise, so it keeps changing? Why?
  3. A popular influencer wants to work with your brand, but her style doesn't match yours at all. Would you take the exposure? Why?
  4. In June, many companies switch to rainbow logos. When is that genuine support, and when is it just marketing? Who should decide?

Docket 7

  1. Which brand's adverts do you actually enjoy? What is it about their tone or style?
  2. Can a brand be both cheap and cool? Think of an example and explain how it manages.
  3. Your packaging is beautiful but expensive; cheaper packaging would mean a lower price. Which matters more to your brand, and why?
  4. Only whisky made in Scotland may be called Scotch. Should every product named after a place be protected like that? Who gains and who loses?

Docket 8

  1. Think of a brand your parents use that you would never buy, or the other way round. What explains the difference?
  2. Is it a brand's job to be liked by everyone, or is being disliked by some people a sign of a strong identity?
  3. Your brand's founder makes a controversial comment online. Do you defend them, distance the brand, or say nothing? Why?
  4. When a brand becomes a symbol for a group of people, who should decide what it stands for: the company or the group? Why?
