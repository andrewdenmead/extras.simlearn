Populism and the generations

Young and old often vote very differently, and new parties keep shaking up old systems. Pick a docket, talk it through with your neighbour, and give reasons for your views.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. When did you last feel that politicians were talking about people like you, or ignoring you? What was the issue?
  2. Older people vote far more often than younger ones. What does that mean for which issues get attention?
  3. A party with a perfect programme and no chance, or a flawed one that could actually win: which would get your vote, and why?
  4. Politicians often claim to speak for 'ordinary people'. Who usually gets left out of that phrase, and does it matter?

Docket 2

  1. Which issue would make you join a party or a protest: housing, climate, jobs or something else? Why that one?
  2. Will your generation be better or worse off than your parents'? What makes you say so?
  3. A brand-new party run by people your age, or an established party with decades of experience: which would you trust in government?
  4. Is it fair for older generations to keep benefits that younger ones may never get? What would a pensioner say to you?

Docket 3

  1. Think of a politician who 'says what people think'. What do you like or dislike about the way they speak?
  2. When does 'speaking plainly' become oversimplifying? Give an example from politics or from your own life.
  3. A leader who keeps every election promise, even bad ones, or one who breaks promises when the facts change: which would you vote for?
  4. If young and old voters want opposite things, how should a democracy decide whose needs come first?

Docket 4

  1. Do you talk politics with your grandparents or older relatives? Where do you agree, and where don't you?
  2. Is the gap between young and old voters now bigger than the gap between rich and poor? What makes you think so?
  3. Cheaper housing for the young or protected pensions for the old: if a government could only do one, which should it choose?
  4. Can a movement that starts as an angry protest become a responsible government? What would it have to give up?

Docket 5

  1. What would make you cast a protest vote, for a party you didn't really like, just to send a message?
  2. Why do you think so few people under 30 sit in the Bundestag or the House of Commons?
  3. A minister with twenty years' experience, or one with fresh ideas and no experience: who would you rather have running a ministry?
  4. Big promises win elections. Which is the bigger danger in government: changing too much too fast, or too little too slowly?

Docket 6

  1. Which political slogan, from any country, has stuck in your head? Why do you think it worked?
  2. Does social media make young people more political, or just angrier? Why?
  3. Young politicians who rent, or older politicians who've seen many crises: which would you trust more to fix housing?
  4. Should parties promise voters what they want, or tell them hard truths they don't want to hear? Which wins more often, and why?

Docket 7

  1. What's one thing you'd change tomorrow if you ran the country? Who would be angry with you?
  2. Why do voters often trust 'outsider' politicians more than career politicians, even when the outsiders have no experience?
  3. Clear winners under first-past-the-post, or coalitions under proportional voting: which system would you choose, and what does each cost?
  4. When politics feels stuck, is shaking it up worth the risk of breaking something? What would a cautious voter say to you?

Docket 8

  1. Who in your life talks most about politics, and does it make you more interested or less?
  2. Is anger a useful force in politics, or does it usually make things worse? Give an example.
  3. A government that does what most people want, or one that does what experts say is best: which would you rather live under?
  4. What would happen to a country if every generation voted only for its own interests? Is that already happening?
