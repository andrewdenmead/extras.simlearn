Warm-up · EIM · C1

Brands and identity

What a brand stands for, and what it says about the people who buy it. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Have you ever bought something mainly because of the brand rather than the product? What was it, and do you regret it?
  2. Which brands are 'cool' right now even though their products aren't really better? What makes them cool?
  3. Which would you rather own: a cheap copy that looks exactly like a famous design, or a plain product from a brand you love? Why?
  4. Some brands are popular mainly because people want to be seen using them. Is that a healthy reason for a brand to succeed? What would its marketing team say?

Docket 2

  1. Which foreign brand is so successful in Germany that it almost feels German? How do you think it managed that?
  2. Do younger people care more about what brands say about them than older people do? Why might that be?
  3. Would you rather wear a brand everyone recognises, or one only people 'in the know' recognise? What does each say about you?
  4. International chains are everywhere. Do they make cities more convenient, or make every city look the same? What would a Berlin Späti owner say?

Docket 3

  1. What does your own style (clothes, room, phone case) say about you? Is that message deliberate?
  2. Some people think sportswear brands are now more about fashion than sport. Which sports brand has the clearest identity, and why?
  3. Would you rather work for a company with a strong identity everyone must fit, or a loose one where anything goes? Who thrives in each?
  4. When a company is bought by a foreign owner, people often say it 'lost its soul'. What do they actually mean, and are they right?

Docket 4

  1. Which product or brand from your home country should be available in Germany? What would Germans need to understand about it first?
  2. Why do some brands reinvent themselves successfully while others fail? Think of one example of each.
  3. Which matters most to you when buying furniture or clothes: quality, price, appearance, sustainability or brand image? Pick two and defend them.
  4. People now buy the same products all over the world. Is the world losing interesting differences, or simply sharing good ideas? What would someone who disagrees say?

Docket 5

  1. Tell your partner about an object you own that you would never throw away. What makes it special?
  2. Which brand has a personality you'd recognise even with the logo removed? How does it do that?
  3. Would you rather follow a brand that never changes its logo, or one that refreshes it regularly? What does each tell customers?
  4. Should a company's values be the same everywhere in the world, even where customers think differently? What would a local manager in another country say?

Docket 6

  1. Which brand did you love as a teenager but have since outgrown? What changed: you or the brand?
  2. Some people pay for 'quiet luxury' with no visible logo. Is that less about showing off, or just a smarter way of showing off?
  3. Would you rather buy a coat that lasts twenty years but costs a lot, or a cheap one you replace when your taste changes? Why?
  4. Companies are now expected to have a 'purpose' as well as products. Does that make them more trustworthy, or just better at marketing?

Docket 7

  1. When you travel abroad, which brands from your own country do you notice? Do they feel different there?
  2. Is a brand's identity created by the company, or by the people who buy it? Think of a brand whose customers changed what it meant.
  3. Would you rather start a company with a big, clear identity from day one, or let it grow naturally from what customers want? What are the risks of each?
  4. Football clubs, universities and even cities now talk about their 'brand'. Is it helpful to think of them that way, or does it change what they are?

Docket 8

  1. Which app, shop or brand do you use every day without really thinking about it? Would you notice if it disappeared?
  2. How do you feel when a small brand you like suddenly becomes extremely popular? Does it lose something, or is that simply success?
  3. Would you rather work for a famous brand with a strong reputation, or an unknown start-up where you could shape its identity? Why?
  4. How different is your identity at work or university from your identity at home? Should employers expect people to fit the company culture, or adapt to them?
