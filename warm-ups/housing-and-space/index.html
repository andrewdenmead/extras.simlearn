Housing and space

Homes are scarce in many cities, but space isn't shared out evenly. Pick a docket, talk it through with your neighbour, and give reasons for your views.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. How long did it take you to find your current room or flat? What was the strangest thing you saw while searching?
  2. Why do you think Berlin's housing shortage has lasted so long, despite years of promises?
  3. A tiny flat of your own in the centre, or a big room in a shared flat out in Spandau: which would you choose, and why?
  4. Should housing be an investment that rises in value, or something that stays affordable? Can it be both, and who would disagree?

Docket 2

  1. How much space do you really need to be happy at home? Has your answer changed since you left your family home?
  2. Many people stay in large homes after their children leave. What keeps them there?
  3. Rent controls that keep prices down but slow new building, or a free market that builds more but costs more: which would you choose for Berlin?
  4. When some households have rooms to spare and others have no home, whose problem is that to solve? What would a homeowner say?

Docket 3

  1. Have you ever shared a flat with people you didn't choose? What made it work, or fail?
  2. Berlin limits turning flats into holiday lets. How far should a city limit what owners do with their own property?
  3. Rent all your life and stay flexible, or buy a small flat with a big loan: which would you choose, and why?
  4. Who should get priority for affordable homes: key workers like nurses, young families, or whoever has waited longest? Why?

Docket 4

  1. Would you ever take a lodger or an extra flatmate to cut your costs? What would your house rules be?
  2. Why do rents in cities like Berlin, Munich or Edinburgh rise faster than wages?
  3. High-rise flats everywhere, or a city that keeps its character but stays expensive: which would you rather live in?
  4. Is it fair that people who bought homes decades ago are now far richer than young renters who work just as hard? What would the owners say?

Docket 5

  1. What was the biggest room in the home you grew up in, and how often was it actually used?
  2. Should families get priority for larger flats over single people or couples? Why?
  3. A small flat with a short commute, or a bigger one with an hour on the S-Bahn each way: which would you choose?
  4. How much should governments interfere in the housing market? Where would you draw the line, and who would push back?

Docket 6

  1. What did you give up to afford where you live now: space, location, quiet or something else?
  2. Why do so many people want to own rather than rent, even in Germany, where renting is normal?
  3. Build homes on Tempelhofer Feld, or keep it open for everyone: which would you choose, and what would the other side say?
  4. Should landlords be free to choose their tenants, or should flats go to whoever needs them most? Who would disagree?

Docket 7

  1. If you could design your ideal home for the next ten years, how many rooms would it have, and what for?
  2. Should housing be a human right? What would that mean in practice?
  3. Student halls with cheap rent and strict rules, or an expensive WG with total freedom: which would you choose, and why?
  4. Should older generations help younger ones onto the housing ladder? How fair is it that some families can and others can't?

Docket 8

  1. Have you ever had to move because of the rent or a landlord's decision? How did it change things for you?
  2. Why is new housing so often opposed by the people who already live nearby?
  3. A flat in a lively area that's slowly gentrifying, or a cheaper one in a quiet area nobody talks about: which would you choose?
  4. In twenty years, will today's housing crisis look like a failure of markets or of governments? What would someone who disagrees say?
