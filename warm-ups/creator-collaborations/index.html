Warm-up · EBC · C1

Creator collaborations

Creators no longer just review products: they design them, sell them and put their name on them. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk about trust, money and honesty when creators and brands work together.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which creator or influencer do you follow most closely? Would you buy a product they designed? Why, or why not?
  2. Prime, the drink launched by two YouTubers, sold out everywhere and then quickly lost popularity. What does that tell you about creator products?
  3. A brand offers you, a student creator, a product with your name on it. What would you insist on before saying yes?
  4. When a creator sells products to fans who trust them, where does friendship end and business begin? Who should protect the fans?

Docket 2

  1. How can you tell when a creator really likes a product, and when they're just being paid? Give an example.
  2. In Germany, influencers must label paid content as 'Werbung'. How does that label change the way you watch a video?
  3. A creator you follow designs a product you don't like. Would you tell them honestly in the comments, or stay quiet? Why?
  4. Should brands be allowed to work with child influencers? What rules, if any, would you set?

Docket 3

  1. What have you bought because a creator recommended it? How well did it live up to what they said?
  2. Do creators who show their mistakes and failures seem more trustworthy to you? Why do you think that works?
  3. You're a brand manager. The creator wants creative freedom; your boss wants a perfect, controlled message. How would you find a middle ground?
  4. Creators earn a living from their audience's attention. Is turning fans into customers fair, or does it exploit their trust?

Docket 4

  1. If you were a creator, what product would you design with a brand? Who would buy it?
  2. Is a creator-designed product better than one designed by a company's own experts, or just better marketed?
  3. You pay a creator to review your product honestly, and the review is mostly negative. What would you do next?
  4. How responsible should creators be if a product they promoted turns out to be poor, compared with the company that made it?

Docket 5

  1. Which creator's style feels most 'real' to you? What do they do that others don't?
  2. Do creators lose their independence once they start selling their own products? What changes in their videos?
  3. You're asked to film a sponsored video that must look 'unplanned'. Would you agree? How would you keep it honest?
  4. How should YouTube and TikTok share advertising money with creators? What would be fair, and why?

Docket 6

  1. Have you ever unfollowed someone because they became too commercial? What was the last straw?
  2. Would you trust a product more if it took a creator a year to design rather than a month? Why?
  3. A brand wants you to film in a beautiful place you know will be ruined by crowds if the video goes viral. What would you do?
  4. Are creators the new celebrities, or something different? What responsibilities come with that kind of influence?

Docket 7

  1. What is the most useful thing you've learned from a creator's video? Did it lead you to buy anything?
  2. Which feels more honest: a creator selling their own merchandise or a creator promoting someone else's product? Why?
  3. Your brand is launching a product designed with a creator. Launch day is close, and the creator wants to change one detail. What would you do?
  4. Should governments regulate creator marketing as strictly as TV advertising? What would we gain and lose?

Docket 8

  1. Which sports, gaming or hobby creator would you trust to recommend equipment? What makes their opinion valuable?
  2. Is 'creator' a real job with real skills, or mostly luck and good timing? What would a creator say?
  3. Your creator product sells out on the first day, and fans who missed it are angry. How would you respond?
  4. When fans buy a creator's product, are they buying the product or a piece of the relationship? What does that mean for brands?
