Warm-up · EBC · C1

Market entry

Entering a new market, with a new brand or in a new country, is exciting and expensive, and famous names don't guarantee success. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk about what it takes to arrive and stay.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Have you ever bought, or avoided, a product because a celebrity was connected to it? What did you expect?
  2. When celebrities launch businesses, how involved do you think they really are? Does it matter to you?
  3. Walmart entered Germany in 1997 and left in 2006. Why do you think some foreign companies struggle here while others succeed?
  4. Should celebrities be held responsible when a product with their name on it disappoints customers? How much responsibility?

Docket 2

  1. Which foreign brand has opened in Berlin recently? Did you try it, and did it live up to the excitement?
  2. Does putting a famous name on a product make it more desirable, less trustworthy, or neither? Give an example.
  3. Your new brand can launch in one country and get it right, or in five at once and learn fast. Which would you choose, and why?
  4. Is it fair that famous people can enter almost any market overnight while small founders struggle for years? What does that do to competition?

Docket 3

  1. What makes you trust a new brand you have never heard of? Think of one you tried recently.
  2. If a product is built around one person's image, should that person have the final say on every major decision? Why?
  3. Your brand goes viral before it's ready to sell. Would you take pre-orders, open a waiting list, or stay quiet? What could go wrong?
  4. How strict should the rules be when celebrities make claims about beauty products? Who should check those claims?

Docket 4

  1. Which celebrity product do you think is actually good? What makes it more than just a famous name?
  2. Which matters most when you try a new beauty or fashion product: the price, the ingredients, or who is behind it?
  3. A famous singer asks you to run her new company. What one condition would you insist on before saying yes, and why?
  4. Why do so many celebrity brands disappear after two or three years? Whose fault is it: the celebrity, the business partners, or the fans?

Docket 5

  1. Think of a brand that changed its products or adverts to fit German customers. What did it change, and did it work?
  2. How much damage can one viral video do to a new brand? Can a company realistically prepare for that?
  3. You're entering a new country. Would you hire local managers who know the market or send people who know your company? Why?
  4. Should countries protect their home markets from big foreign brands, for example to save local shops? Who would gain and lose?

Docket 6

  1. Which celebrity, athlete or creator would you trust to recommend a product to you? Why them?
  2. Would you pay more for a product designed by a celebrity you admire? How much more, and why?
  3. A new market wants a slightly different product from your home market. Do you adapt it or keep it the same everywhere? What do you risk?
  4. Many celebrity beauty brands are aimed at teenagers. Should there be special rules for marketing to young fans? What kind?

Docket 7

  1. Have you ever joined a queue or a waiting list for a new product? What made it worth it, or not?
  2. When a celebrity brand fails, should the celebrity apologise personally, or is it just business? What do fans expect?
  3. You can launch your new brand online only or in physical shops. Which builds more trust for a newcomer, and why?
  4. Should companies avoid markets whose culture they don't understand, or is learning by doing the only way? What would each side argue?

Docket 8

  1. What is the most surprising product a famous person has launched? What would make you buy it?
  2. Which is riskier for a new brand: growing too fast or growing too slowly? Give an example.
  3. A famous name gets your brand meetings with every big retailer. How would you prove your brand is more than just the name?
  4. Do celebrity brands push smaller, independent brands off the shelves? What, if anything, should shops do about it?
