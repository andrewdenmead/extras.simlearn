Migration and welcome

Who moves, who welcomes them, and on what terms? Pick a docket, talk it through with your neighbour, and give reasons for your views.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Have you ever lived somewhere where you didn't speak the language well? What helped you most, and what made it harder?
  2. Berlin is full of people from somewhere else. What makes a newcomer start to feel like a Berliner, and how long does it take?
  3. A high salary and a cold welcome, or a lower salary and a warm one: which country would you rather move to, and why?
  4. What do newcomers owe the place they move to, and what does that place owe them? What would someone who disagrees say?

Docket 2

  1. Who in your family has moved country or region, and why? How do they talk about it now?
  2. Why are some migrants called 'expats' and others 'immigrants'? Who decides which word is used?
  3. Learn the language before you arrive, or arrive first and learn it on the job: which works better, and for whom?
  4. Is it fair that a passport you didn't choose decides where you may live and work? What would a defender of borders say?

Docket 3

  1. What surprised you most the first time you dealt with bureaucracy in Germany or in another country?
  2. What shapes people's views on migration most: meeting migrants, the media or their own family history? Why?
  3. Should newcomers be free to choose any city to live in, or be placed where there are homes and jobs? Why?
  4. Millions of Germans emigrated to America in the 1800s. Does a country's own history of emigration create any duty towards today's migrants?

Docket 4

  1. Think of a time you were the outsider: a new class, a new job, a new city. What did others do that made a difference?
  2. Which matters most for settling into a new country: a job, a friend or the language? Why?
  3. Admit fewer people and support them well, or admit more with less support each: which would you choose, and why?
  4. Should newcomers adopt local customs, or should the local culture adapt too? Give an example from Berlin or somewhere else.

Docket 5

  1. What's one thing about German life you'd tell a newcomer to adopt immediately, and one they can safely ignore?
  2. How much say should a country's citizens have over how many people arrive each year, and how should they have it?
  3. Citizenship after five years with language and knowledge tests, or after eight years with no tests: which is fairer, and why?
  4. Can a country be generous to newcomers and still keep public support for migration? What would it take, and what would a sceptic say?

Docket 6

  1. Have you noticed people treating you differently when you speak another language or have an accent? How?
  2. Is it easier to welcome newcomers in a big city like Berlin or in a small town? Why?
  3. A newcomer who works hard but never learns German, or one who speaks fluent German but can't find work: who is better integrated?
  4. Is migration mainly a problem to be managed or an opportunity to be used? What would someone with the opposite view say?

Docket 7

  1. If you had to move abroad tomorrow for five years, where would you go, and what would you miss most?
  2. Why do you think migration debates in Germany and the UK sound so similar, even though the two countries' histories differ?
  3. Temporary work visas that end after five years, or a clear path to staying for good: which is better for the workers, and for the country?
  4. Do people have a right to move for a better life, or do countries have a right to say no? How would you balance the two?

Docket 8

  1. What did you have to sort out first when you moved to Berlin or to university: a flat, a bank account, registration? What was hardest?
  2. Should people who live somewhere be able to vote in local elections before they become citizens? What difference would it make?
  3. A neighbourhood festival or a compulsory integration course: which would do more to bring locals and newcomers together, and why?
  4. In fifty years, how will people judge the way Europe treats migrants today? What would someone who disagrees with you predict?
