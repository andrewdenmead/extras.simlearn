Warm-up · EFD · B2/C1

Design and storytelling

Brands, products, cities and apps all tell stories. Who tells them, and who gets to decide what they mean? Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which brand has a really strong personality for you? If it were a person, what would it be like?
  2. Some objects seem to carry a story, like a flea-market lamp or a grandparent's watch. Can a designer create that feeling on purpose?
  3. For your portfolio, would you show your best project as a clean success story, or include your mistakes and dead ends? Why?
  4. Who controls the meaning of a design in the end: the designer, the client or the audience? Give an example.

Docket 2

  1. What's the last story, in a film, ad, book or post, that really moved you? What made it work?
  2. In the UK, some Christmas adverts are almost a national event. Why do people love brand stories they know are made to sell?
  3. You're telling a bank's story: humour or emotion? Which would you choose, and which is harder to get right?
  4. Is it fair for brands to use social causes like climate or equality as part of their story? When does it become exploitation?

Docket 3

  1. Do you prefer brands with a long history or brands that feel completely new? Why?
  2. IKEA gives its products Swedish names. How much story does a name carry? Would you care if the names were random?
  3. An exhibition about your city: would you tell its story through famous people or ordinary residents? Which would visitors remember?
  4. When a story about a country, city or group is told by outsiders, who gets to decide whether it's accurate?

Docket 4

  1. Think of an app or product with a mascot or character. Does the character make you like the product more?
  2. Huge companies love telling 'underdog' stories about themselves. Is it honest for a big company to tell its story as if it were small?
  3. Your client wants a story that makes everyone smile; you think a slightly sad story would be more memorable. Which would you push for?
  4. Does a good story make us forgive a product for being worse or more expensive? Is that a problem, and for whom?

Docket 5

  1. Which film, game or series has a visual world you'd recognise instantly? What design choices create it?
  2. Why do football clubs' stories make fans so loyal? Could a design brand ever create that kind of loyalty?
  3. Would you rather tell a story with words and images, or with images alone? Which is harder for an audience to misunderstand?
  4. Charts and maps in the news tell stories with data. When does a visual story about data become misleading?

Docket 6

  1. What family or local story would you love to see turned into a design: a poster, a book, an exhibition?
  2. Why do so many companies say they are 'on a mission'? Do you believe them? What would make you believe them?
  3. A charity can show the people it helps as heroes or as victims. Which would you choose for its campaign, and what's the risk of each?
  4. Should companies be allowed to tell their story in schools, for example through sponsored teaching materials? Who benefits?

Docket 7

  1. What story does your own style, like your clothes, room or phone case, tell about you? Is it the story you want?
  2. A happy ending or an open ending: which makes a brand story more memorable? Why?
  3. A festival poster: show the crowd having fun, or the artists and the music? What story does each tell, and who is it for?
  4. When a story spreads online, people change and remix it. Should the original creator still control what it means?

Docket 8

  1. Which advert or campaign recently made you laugh or cry? Did you remember the brand afterwards?
  2. Some apps tell a cute story while you sign up, with mascots and jokes. Charming, or a waste of your time? When would you skip it?
  3. For a new product, would you build the story around what it does or how it makes people feel? Which lasts longer?
  4. Edinburgh and Berlin are both sold to tourists through a few famous images. Who benefits from that story, and who gets left out?
