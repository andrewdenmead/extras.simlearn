Design and localisation

What should change when a design travels to another country, and what should stay the same? Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Have you ever seen a familiar brand look or taste different in another country? What surprised you?
  2. Would you rather watch a foreign film dubbed or with subtitles? What does each gain and lose?
  3. Film studios sometimes change scenes or characters so a film can be shown in big markets abroad. Smart business, or giving away creative control?
  4. When a global brand adapts to local culture, is that respect or just a smarter way to sell? What would a sceptic say?

Docket 2

  1. What product name, slogan or logo sounds strange or funny in your language?
  2. McDonald's menus change from country to country. Which parts of a brand should never change, and which can?
  3. You're adapting an ad campaign full of jokes for another country. Translate the jokes closely, or replace them with local ones? Why?
  4. Should a company change its product to fit local customs it disagrees with? Where would you draw the line?

Docket 3

  1. Which colours or symbols mean something different in another culture you know? Have you seen a misunderstanding?
  2. Is English slowly becoming the default language of design, even in Berlin? Is that a problem, and for whom?
  3. A Berlin start-up launches in Spain: hire a local Spanish agency, or use its own team who know the brand? What's the risk of each?
  4. When streaming platforms make local content for every country, does global culture become richer or more similar? Defend your view.

Docket 4

  1. Do you use your apps in English or in German? Why that language?
  2. Why do some designs travel well, like IKEA's wordless instructions, while others fail abroad?
  3. Packaging for 20 countries: one design with many languages, or a separate design for each country? Which would you choose, and why?
  4. Should designers working abroad use local stereotypes if local audiences like them? Who decides what's offensive?

Docket 5

  1. Has an app, sign or website in another country ever confused you because of different conventions, like dates, money or addresses?
  2. Some brands use the same advert everywhere and just change the voice. Is that efficient or lazy?
  3. A Scottish shortbread brand wants to sell in Japan. Which parts of its look would you keep, and which might not travel? Why?
  4. Small companies can't afford to adapt their design for every market. Does that make localisation unfair? Who does it favour?

Docket 6

  1. Which film, game or series was changed for your country: a new title, cut scenes, a different poster? Did it matter?
  2. Döner kebab in Berlin is different from kebab in Turkey. When a tradition is adapted abroad, is it still the 'real' thing? Does it matter?
  3. Would you choose a brand name that's easy to say everywhere, or one with real meaning in one language? Why?
  4. Machine translation is now very good. Will we still need human translators and local designers in ten years? What would one of them say?

Docket 7

  1. How would you explain a Berlin habit, like Pfand, Späti culture or Sunday closing, to a visitor? What would they find strangest?
  2. Emojis look different on different phones and mean different things to different groups. Can a symbol ever be truly universal?
  3. One website for all of Europe, or separate ones for each country? What would each cost the company and its customers?
  4. Should global companies hire mainly local designers in each market, even if it costs more and slows decisions? Who benefits?

Docket 8

  1. Which product or brand from your home country would you love to see abroad? What would have to change?
  2. Some drinks brands print popular first names on their bottles. What happens when your name isn't one they print?
  3. You're adapting a Berlin bike-sharing app for Glasgow. What would you change beyond the language? Think of weather, hills and habits.
  4. Does localisation help people feel at home, or does it stop them experiencing other cultures? Defend your view.
