Warm-up · EFD · B2/C1

Packaging design

Packaging protects, sells and ends up in the bin. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which product have you bought mainly because of its packaging? Did the product live up to the box?
  2. Some crisp packets are half air. Is that protecting the product, or tricking the shopper? Where's the line?
  3. Would you pay 50 cents more for a product in plastic-free packaging, even if it's less convenient to store? What would most people do?
  4. Should Germany's Pfand idea, a deposit you get back, be used for more kinds of packaging, not just bottles? Who would gain and who would lose?

Docket 2

  1. What's the most annoying packaging you've ever tried to open? How would you redesign it?
  2. Some phone boxes are designed to make opening them feel special. Is a great unboxing experience worth the extra material?
  3. Your client's product sits on a crowded supermarket shelf. Would you make it loud and colourful or calm and minimal? What's the risk of each?
  4. Some supermarkets now sell pasta, rice and soap from refill stations. Should these replace packaged versions, even if shopping takes longer? Who would complain?

Docket 3

  1. Do you ever keep packaging, like a tin, jar or box, after the product is finished? Why that one?
  2. What makes packaging look 'healthy' or 'natural'? Can design make a product seem healthier than it really is?
  3. A cereal box: big and eye-catching on the shelf, or smaller with less waste? Which would you design, and who benefits from each?
  4. Should food packaging have to show its environmental impact clearly, like a traffic-light label? Would shoppers actually change what they buy?

Docket 4

  1. Think of the Späti or supermarket where you shop. Which drink or snack is easiest to find, and why?
  2. Do heavy glass, matte finishes and gold foil make you believe a product tastes better? Why might that happen?
  3. Lots of text explaining the product, or almost none and let the design speak: which would you choose for a new food product?
  4. Are bright, cartoon-style sweet packets unfair to children and parents? Should there be rules, and what would a sweet company say?

Docket 5

  1. How closely do you look at recycling symbols on packaging? Do you understand them all?
  2. Why do so many craft-beer cans or skincare products look almost the same? Is following a style a smart idea or a mistake?
  3. Shampoo in a beautiful bottle you'll throw away, or a refill in a plain pouch: which would you buy, and what would your friends choose?
  4. If greener packaging makes food more expensive, who should pay: shoppers, brands, supermarkets or governments? Defend your answer.

Docket 6

  1. Have you ever been disappointed when you opened a package? Was there less inside, or did it look different from the picture?
  2. Food photos on packaging rarely look like the real thing. Is 'serving suggestion' an honest excuse or a design trick?
  3. Your budget allows a great structural design (shape, opening) or great graphics, but not both. Which would you choose, and why?
  4. Online shopping creates mountains of cardboard. Should online shops have to use reusable boxes, even if delivery gets more expensive?

Docket 7

  1. Which product could you recognise in a second from its packaging, even without the name? What makes it so recognisable?
  2. Does packaging matter less now that so many people shop online and only see a small photo? Why or why not?
  3. For a new snack, would you design the packaging mainly for the shelf or mainly for Instagram photos? Explain your choice.
  4. Should there be a tax on packaging that is mainly decorative? What would a luxury brand reply?

Docket 8

  1. Do you usually buy supermarket own brands or famous brands? How much does the packaging influence you?
  2. Some packaging seems designed to be impossible to resist when you're tired or hungry. Is that good design or manipulation?
  3. Two job offers: packaging for a famous global brand, or for a small zero-waste start-up. Which would you take, and why?
  4. People in Germany sort their rubbish carefully, but much of it still isn't recycled. Who should carry more responsibility: designers or consumers?
