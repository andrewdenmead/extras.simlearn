Warm-up · EFD · B2/C1

Form vs function

Beautiful, practical, or both? Pick a docket with your neighbour, talk through all four prompts and give reasons for your answers.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Think of something you own that looks great but is annoying to use. Why did you buy it, and do you still use it?
  2. The Apple Magic Mouse charges through a port underneath, so you can't use it while it charges. Can a badly designed product still deserve its success?
  3. A kitchen chair: beautiful but uncomfortable after an hour, or comfortable but forgettable. Which would sell better in Berlin, and why?
  4. A furniture chain sells a cheap copy of a famous chair: it looks identical but is less comfortable. Is it still the same design? Who loses?

Docket 2

  1. What's the most practical object you use every day that you'd never call beautiful? Would you change how it looks if you could?
  2. Crocs were mocked for years, then became a fashion item. Has the shoe changed, or have we? What made the difference?
  3. Your flatmate wants a stylish designer lamp; you want a cheap one that lights the room well. Who should win, and what's a fair compromise?
  4. When a famous design fails ordinary users, who is responsible: the designer, the client who approved it, or the critics who praised it?

Docket 3

  1. Have you ever been unable to work out how to open, switch on or use something? What did the designer get wrong?
  2. The Fiat Multipla was voted one of the ugliest cars ever, yet its six-seat layout was clever and later copied. Can a design fail and succeed at once?
  3. For your final project, would you rather design something that wins a prize or something people use every day for ten years? Why?
  4. Should public objects like ticket machines, bins and bus shelters be designed to be admired, or only to work? What would an architect say?

Docket 4

  1. Which app or website looks good but frustrates you every time you use it? What exactly goes wrong?
  2. Glass-walled offices look open and modern, but many staff say they feel watched and can't concentrate. Should a building's look ever win over how people feel inside?
  3. Your client loves a design you know will be expensive to maintain. Do you push back hard, or deliver what they're paying for? Explain.
  4. Does 'form follows function' still make sense when people buy products mainly to show who they are? Who would disagree, and why?

Docket 5

  1. Think of a building in Berlin you love or hate. Is your opinion about how it looks or how it works for the people inside?
  2. The Tesla Cybertruck is radical and much photographed, but criticised for safety and practicality. Does being different deserve credit on its own?
  3. You own a café: beautiful hard chairs that make people leave sooner, or comfortable ordinary ones. Which would you choose, and what would customers say?
  4. Is ugly but useful design more sustainable, because people keep it longer, or less, because nobody cares enough to look after it?

Docket 6

  1. Have you ever bought something mainly because of how it looked in a photo online? How did it compare in real life?
  2. Google Glass failed partly because people felt silly wearing it in public. Is how a product makes you look part of its function?
  3. Would you rather have a thinner, more beautiful phone, or a thicker one whose battery lasts two days? Who decides this for most people?
  4. Instagram rewards products that photograph well. Is that pushing designers towards objects that look better than they work? What's the counterargument?

Docket 7

  1. What object from your childhood home was ugly but worked perfectly for years? Why do you think it lasted?
  2. Chunky 'ugly sneakers' suddenly became fashionable and expensive. When an ugly design becomes desirable, is it good design or just good timing?
  3. A bike lock for Berlin students: light and elegant, or heavy and much harder to break? Which would you design, and who would buy it?
  4. Should governments set rules on how practical everyday products must be, or should the market decide? What would a designer say?

Docket 8

  1. When you get dressed in the morning, does comfort or appearance usually win? Has that changed since you started university?
  2. The Segway was meant to change how cities move, but ended up mostly with tour groups. Was it a bad product, or a good product in the wrong world?
  3. Same salary: designing beautiful luxury objects, or useful but unglamorous hospital equipment. Which job would you take, and why?
  4. Some city benches have armrests designed to stop people lying down on them. When a design's function is to control people, is it still good design?
