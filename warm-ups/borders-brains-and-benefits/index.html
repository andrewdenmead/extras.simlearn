Warm-up · EIM · C1

Borders, brains and benefits

Skilled people move around the world for work. Who gains, who loses, and who decides? Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Would you consider working abroad after your degree? What would make you go, and what would make you come back?
  2. Germany is short of skilled workers. Where do you notice shortages in daily life: trains, cafés, doctors' appointments, schools?
  3. Would you rather work abroad for a high salary in a job below your qualifications, or at home in your field for much less? Why?
  4. Getting foreign qualifications recognised in Germany can take months or years. Is that protecting quality, or wasting talent? What would each side say?

Docket 2

  1. Do you know someone who moved to another country for work? What was the hardest part of their first year?
  2. Why do you think many young Germans move to Switzerland, the Netherlands or the UK for work? What would bring them back?
  3. Would you rather start a job abroad alongside a group of other newcomers, or alone among locals? What are the advantages of each?
  4. When there's a shortage of workers, should employers recruit from abroad or raise wages to attract local people? Who would argue for each?

Docket 3

  1. Which jobs do you think people in your country no longer want to do? Why not?
  2. Many international students leave Germany soon after graduating. Why, and what could make them stay?
  3. Should your country train more of its own engineers and IT specialists, which is slow and expensive, or recruit them from abroad? What's the catch with each?
  4. Some say people should be free to work anywhere; others say countries need to keep the skills they paid to create. Where do you stand, and why?

Docket 4

  1. Have you ever had to prove your qualifications or language level in another country? How did the process feel?
  2. Is speaking the local language more important than professional skills when you start a job abroad? Think of jobs where each matters most.
  3. Would you rather your employer chose and organised your first posting abroad, or let you choose and organise everything yourself? Why?
  4. When a school, hospital or factory is short-staffed, the remaining staff often burn out. Who should fix this: employers, governments or unions?

Docket 5

  1. Which country would you most like to work in for a few years? What do you imagine your daily life there would be like?
  2. Why are some professions, like care work, badly paid even though everyone says they're important?
  3. Would you rather be paid exactly the same as local colleagues from day one, or receive a bonus for moving that locals don't get? How might colleagues react?
  4. Germany's 'guest worker' schemes of the 1960s expected people to go home, but many stayed. What lessons should countries recruiting workers today learn from that?

Docket 6

  1. Tell your partner about the most international workplace or class you've experienced. What made it work, or not?
  2. Brexit made it harder for EU citizens to work in the UK. What effects do you think that has had on British businesses, and on the workers?
  3. Would you rather work in a country with high taxes and strong public services, or one with low taxes where you pay privately for more? Why?
  4. Machines can't easily replace carers, nurses or teachers. How should societies make these jobs attractive enough, and who should pay?

Docket 7

  1. What would you miss most about home if you moved abroad for five years?
  2. Some towns and regions offer money to people who move there to work. Would that tempt you? What would make it worth it?
  3. Which would attract you more to a job abroad: a famous employer with average conditions, or an unknown one with excellent conditions? Why?
  4. When a country can't fill its jobs, should it make immigration easier, persuade people to work longer, or accept fewer services? What would each mean for ordinary people?

Docket 8

  1. Think of someone who came from another country to do their job here: a lecturer, a doctor, a delivery rider. What did you notice about their experience?
  2. Why do some countries find it easy to attract skilled workers, while others struggle even with good salaries?
  3. Would you rather move abroad to a big city like London or Berlin, or to a small Highland town that badly needs people with your skills? Why?
  4. Europe will need many more care workers as people live longer, but fewer young people want those jobs. What should a society do first, and what would critics say?
