Warm-up · EBC · C1

Brand architecture

Many of the brands we buy belong to a handful of big groups, some proudly and some quietly. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk about how companies organise their brands, and what customers do and don't get to know.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which brands do you use that might belong to the same big company? Did you know, and does it matter to you?
  2. Would you trust a luxury car made by a company best known for cheap family cars? What would it have to do to convince you?
  3. Rügenwalder Mühle, famous for sausages, now sells more vegetarian products than meat. Would a meat brand's veggie burger tempt you or put you off? Why?
  4. If one brand in a group is caught in a scandal, should customers boycott the group's other brands too? Who would that hurt?

Docket 2

  1. Which 'family' of products from one company do you know best? Which member of that family is the black sheep, and why?
  2. Meta owns Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. Should their connection be more visible to users? What would change if it were?
  3. Your group owns a fast-fashion brand and a sustainable one. A journalist asks whether the green brand is honest. What would you answer?
  4. Should big groups have to show the parent company's name on every product? Who would gain and who would lose?

Docket 3

  1. Think of a brand that 'grew up' with you. Is it still for you, or has it moved on to younger customers?
  2. Is it sensible for companies to create special 'youth brands', or can young customers tell when older managers are trying too hard?
  3. Supermarket own-brand products are sometimes made in the same factories as famous brands. Would you still pay more for the famous name? Why?
  4. Do too many brands owned by too few companies make shopping a fake choice? What, if anything, should be done about it?

Docket 4

  1. Which supermarket own brand do you buy instead of a famous name? What makes you trust it?
  2. Nestlé owns brands as different as KitKat, Nespresso and pet food. How, if at all, does knowing that change how you see them?
  3. You must name a new product: a name that sounds global or one that sounds local. Which would you choose, and for what kind of product?
  4. Should competition authorities stop big companies buying up rival brands, even if prices stay low? Who really benefits from fewer owners?

Docket 5

  1. If you could take one brand away from a big company and run it yourself, which would it be? What would you change first?
  2. Volkswagen owns Škoda, SEAT, Porsche and Lamborghini. How different do these brands really need to be, and why?
  3. Your company wants to sell a cheaper version of its premium product. Same name, a new name, or don't do it at all? Defend your choice.
  4. Many 'independent' craft beers and organic brands now belong to global groups. Should that be clear on the pack? Why?

Docket 6

  1. Which brand has so many versions or spin-offs that you get confused? What would you tell its managers?
  2. Which brand do you stay loyal to even though a cheaper, almost identical option exists? What exactly are you paying for?
  3. You're offered a job at a small, cool brand, then learn it belongs to a company you dislike. Would you still take it? Why?
  4. Should a group be allowed to use the same advertising tricks for its 'healthy' brands and its 'unhealthy' ones? Where's the line?

Docket 7

  1. Which advert or logo do you connect with your childhood? Does that memory still affect what you buy?
  2. Should fast-food brands look and feel the same in every country, or adapt to local tastes? Why?
  3. A group has a budget brand and a premium brand. A customer finds almost the same product in both, at very different prices. How would you explain it?
  4. Should a company's brands share their customers' data with each other? What would customers gain, and what would they give up?

Docket 8

  1. Which brand would surprise you most if you learned it was owned by a much bigger company? How would you find out?
  2. Why do so many big companies buy small brands instead of creating their own? What does the small brand gain or lose?
  3. Your group owns a premium cosmetics brand and a cheap one. Should the cheap brand be allowed to copy the premium one's best product? Why?
  4. Coca-Cola owns brands that sell water, juice and sugary drinks. Is that a healthy mix, or a way to win whatever customers choose?
