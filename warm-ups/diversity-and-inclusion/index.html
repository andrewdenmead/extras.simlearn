Warm-up · EIM · C1/C2

Diversity and inclusion

Who gets in, who feels they belong, and who decides what 'fair' means. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Think of a group, class or job where you felt like an outsider at first. What helped you feel included, or what didn't?
  2. Is treating everyone exactly the same always fair? Describe a situation where it might not be.
  3. Would you rather work in a team of close friends who think alike, or strangers from very different backgrounds? What would each team be good and bad at?
  4. Should companies take social class as seriously as gender, ethnicity or disability? How would they even notice it?

Docket 2

  1. What 'unwritten rules' did you have to learn when you started university or a new job? Who explained them, if anyone?
  2. If almost all senior managers in a company come from one group, what explanations might there be? Which would you investigate first?
  3. For an international team meeting, would you choose English only, so everyone's equal, or each person's own language with translation tools? Who wins and who loses?
  4. Many workplaces hold Christmas parties but few mark other festivals. How should an international company handle holidays fairly? What would someone who disagrees say?

Docket 3

  1. Have you ever been judged by your accent, your age or where you're from? How did it affect you?
  2. Some people say humour brings teams together; others say it often excludes people. When does a joke at work cross a line?
  3. Would you rather your employer offered flexible hours to everyone, or extra support specifically for parents and carers? Who might feel left out in each case?
  4. International companies work across cultures with very different views on equality. Should they apply the same rules in every country, or adapt? What would a local manager say?

Docket 4

  1. Which role models in sport, film, business or online did you look up to growing up? What made them inspiring to you?
  2. Some Berlin U-Bahn stations still have no lift. How would you design a city that works for everyone, and who should pay?
  3. Would you prefer a workplace where everyone dresses similarly, or one where anything goes? What does each say about who 'belongs'?
  4. Some say diverse teams are more creative; others say communication gets harder. Which effect have you noticed, and how could a team get the benefits without the problems?

Docket 5

  1. In your university groups, do people tend to work with people like themselves? Why does that happen?
  2. Why do you think some groups are still under-represented in fields like engineering, nursing or finance? Where does it start?
  3. Would you rather have a manager who is very like you, or one who is very different? What could you learn from each?
  4. Older workers are often overlooked for jobs and training. Should companies make a special effort to keep them, or focus on young talent? Who benefits from each?

Docket 6

  1. Tell your partner about a time someone made a wrong assumption about you. How did you react?
  2. Which matters more: a workplace that is diverse, or one that is inclusive? Can a company be one without the other?
  3. Would you rather work remotely from anywhere, or in an office where you see colleagues every day? Who does remote work help, and who does it leave out?
  4. Many companies ask staff to do unconscious-bias training. Can a short course really change how people behave? What else might work better?

Docket 7

  1. What's the most international team or class you've been in? What surprised you about working together?
  2. Students whose parents never went to university often find the start harder. What small things could a university do to help?
  3. Would you rather your university gave scholarships based on grades, or on financial need? Who would each choice help most?
  4. Disabled people are much less likely to be employed. What should employers be responsible for, and what should the state pay for? What would a small business owner say?

Docket 8

  1. Which language do you dream, joke or argue in? What does that tell you about language and belonging?
  2. Do you think men and women are treated differently in group projects or at work? What have you noticed?
  3. Would you rather be the only person like you in a well-paid team, or one of many in a lower-paid team? Why?
  4. Some argue that pay rules should make salaries fully transparent; others say pay is a private matter. Which would make workplaces fairer, and who might lose out?
