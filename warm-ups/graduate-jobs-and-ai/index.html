Graduate jobs and AI

AI is changing the kind of work that graduates start their careers with. Pick a docket, talk it through with your neighbour, and give reasons for your views.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which tasks in your studies or job would you happily hand to AI tomorrow? Which would you keep, and why?
  2. Why do some employers hire graduates of any subject, while others insist on a specific degree? Which approach makes more sense to you?
  3. A degree and years of study, or a paid Ausbildung in a trade from day one: which would you advise a younger sibling to choose today?
  4. If AI makes professional advice far cheaper for everyone, is that worth fewer jobs for graduates? Who wins and who loses?

Docket 2

  1. What did you learn in a job or internship that no lecture could have taught you?
  2. Why do employers still ask for degrees, when they could test skills directly?
  3. A firm that uses AI heavily and pays well, or one that uses little AI and trains you slowly: which would you join, and why?
  4. When technology makes some skills worthless, how much should society protect the people who spent years learning them? Who would disagree?

Docket 3

  1. How do you use AI in your studies right now? Has it made you better at your subject, or just faster?
  2. Will your degree mean as much in ten years as your parents' qualifications meant to them? Why?
  3. Learning to do a task properly yourself, or learning to supervise an AI doing it: which skill is worth more for your career?
  4. Should universities still teach skills that AI already does well, like summarising or translating? What would a lecturer who disagrees say?

Docket 4

  1. What job did you want at 12, and do you think it will still exist when you're 40?
  2. Which jobs will AI change most in the next five years, and which will it barely touch? Why?
  3. A secure, ordinary job that AI can't do, or an exciting one that AI might take over in ten years: which would you choose?
  4. Should companies have to tell customers when work was done by AI rather than people? Why, and what would firms say?

Docket 5

  1. Have you done a task with AI that would have taken you hours alone? How did it feel: relief, pride or worry?
  2. Why do people often trust a human professional more than a machine, even for routine tasks? When is that trust sensible, and when isn't it?
  3. A good degree or two years of real work experience: which would you rather have on your CV, and why?
  4. Is a job mainly about income, or also about identity and meaning? What would someone who sees work only as money say?

Docket 6

  1. Which part of university has been most useful for the work you want to do, and which has been least useful?
  2. Is it harder for your generation to get a first job than it was for your parents? What's your evidence?
  3. An internship at a famous firm doing mostly admin, or at a small start-up doing real work: which would you take?
  4. German unions and works councils protect workers. How should they respond to AI at work, and what would employers say?

Docket 7

  1. If an AI could write your next essay as well as you, what would still be worth doing yourself?
  2. Why do professions like law and medicine carry so much prestige? Could AI change that?
  3. Less pay in a job that AI makes more interesting, or more pay in one that's mostly supervising machines: which would you choose?
  4. Is it a university's job to make students employable, or only to educate them? Who would disagree, and why?

Docket 8

  1. What's one skill you have that you're sure no AI could replace? How sure are you, really?
  2. Should students be allowed to use AI in exams if they'll use it at work anyway? Why?
  3. A graduate job in a big city with high rents, or remote work from a cheap town with fewer colleagues to learn from: which would you choose?
  4. What do we owe the next generation of workers: jobs, training or simply honest advice? What would someone who disagrees say?
