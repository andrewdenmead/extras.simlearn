Warm-up · EIM · C1

Technology and society

New technology makes life easier, and raises questions nobody had to answer before. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which app or AI tool saves you the most time each week? What do you actually do with the time it saves?
  2. If virtual experiences become as satisfying as real ones, why should people still choose reality?
  3. Would you rather be taught by an excellent AI tutor or an average human teacher? What would you gain and what would you lose?
  4. If a company uses AI to do the work of a hundred people, who should benefit from the savings: shareholders, the remaining staff or customers? What would each say?

Docket 2

  1. Have you ever trusted a technology, like maps, autocorrect or a translation app, and it let you down badly? What happened?
  2. Would you speak freely to someone wearing smart glasses that might be recording or analysing you? How would the conversation change?
  3. Is the convenience of a cashless society worth making every purchase traceable? Which side are you on, and why?
  4. When a self-driving car causes an accident, who should be responsible: the owner, the manufacturer or the software developer? What would each argue?

Docket 3

  1. How do you use AI in your studies? Where do you draw the line between help and cheating?
  2. Should making a convincing fake video of a real person be illegal, even when it's clearly comedy? Where would you draw the line?
  3. Imagine facial recognition replaced tickets on the BVG: you just walk on and pay automatically. Convenient or creepy? What would make you accept it?
  4. Should wealthy people be allowed to buy technology that gives them better brains, bodies or longer lives? What happens to everyone else?

Docket 4

  1. Which job in your family has changed most because of technology in the last ten years? Was the change for better or worse?
  2. Which tasks in your future job would you happily hand to AI, and which would you never give up? Why?
  3. Would you rather your manager were a person who sometimes has bad days, or an AI system that's always consistent but can't be persuaded? Why?
  4. If a brain implant let people learn twice as fast, would students who refused it be at an unfair disadvantage? Who should decide whether it's allowed?

Docket 5

  1. How much of your screen time is chosen by you, and how much by an algorithm? How do you know?
  2. Online reviews, ratings and likes guide many of our choices. How much do you trust them, and what makes you suspicious?
  3. Would you rather use a free app that sells your data, or pay €5 a month for one that doesn't? Be honest about what you'd really do.
  4. Should AI be allowed to make hiring decisions if it were proven to be less biased than humans? What would someone who disagrees say?

Docket 6

  1. Have you ever automated something boring in your own life: a spreadsheet, a reminder, a chatbot? How much time did it really save?
  2. AI can now write emails, code and essays. Which human skill will become more valuable because of this, and why?
  3. Would you rather your train were driven by a human, or fully automated if that were slightly safer? Explain your choice.
  4. If AI makes workers twice as productive, should the working week get shorter, or should companies simply produce twice as much? Who would argue for each?

Docket 7

  1. Which technology did older relatives warn you about? Did it turn out fine, or were they right?
  2. When a machine makes a mistake, people are often less forgiving than when a human does. Why do you think that is?
  3. Would you rather live in a 'smart home' that runs everything for you, or a simple home where you control everything? What would you give up?
  4. Should the companies that build AI pay for the problems it causes, such as misinformation or lost jobs? What would those companies say?

Docket 8

  1. If your phone disappeared for a week, which part of your life would be most difficult? Which part might improve?
  2. AI can already pass many university exams. How should exams change, and what should they test instead?
  3. Would you rather have a job that technology makes easier but more boring, or one that stays hard but interesting? Why?
  4. Some tech companies are bigger than many countries' economies. Who should control how they use AI: governments, users or the companies themselves?
