Warm-up · EBC · C1

Mergers and brand integration

When a big company buys a smaller one, customers, staff and founders all wonder what will change. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk about what happens to a brand, and its people, after a takeover.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which brand you like has been bought by a bigger company? Did anything change for you as a customer?
  2. When Coca-Cola bought Innocent smoothies, some fans felt betrayed. Were they right to feel that way, or is it just business? Explain.
  3. Your small company is bought, and the new owners seem to speak a different business 'language'. What would you do in the first week?
  4. After a takeover, who should the new owners listen to most: customers, staff or shareholders? Why?

Docket 2

  1. Have you ever joined a new team, school or job where everyone already had their own habits? How did you fit in?
  2. Which is harder to bring together after a takeover: the systems and money, or the people and culture? Why?
  3. You love your job at a small firm. A global group buys it and offers you a promotion at its head office abroad. What would you do?
  4. Should governments be able to block foreign takeovers of well-known national companies? Who would gain and who would lose?

Docket 3

  1. How would you feel if your favourite local café or bar were bought by a chain? Would you keep going, and why?
  2. Do customers really care who owns a brand, as long as the product stays the same? What evidence do you see?
  3. You're on a merger team. Staff from the two companies keep complaining about each other. What would you try first?
  4. Many takeovers fail to deliver what was promised. Why do companies keep buying others, and who usually wins in the end?

Docket 4

  1. Have you ever stopped buying a product after learning who owns it? What made the difference?
  2. Is selling your company to a big group a reward for success or a betrayal of the people who supported you?
  3. After a takeover, the new owner wants everyone to use its software, rules and dress code. Which would you accept, and which would you fight?
  4. When a big company buys a much-loved brand, should customers have any say in what happens next? What kind of say?

Docket 5

  1. Think of two companies or brands that merged. Can you tell which one 'won'? How?
  2. Which matters more after a takeover: moving fast to save money, or moving slowly to keep people on board? Why?
  3. Your company has bought a rival with angry fans online. Would you reply to them, ignore them, or invite them in? What could go wrong?
  4. Daimler and Chrysler merged in 1998 and split nine years later. Why do you think culture clashes ruin so many deals?

Docket 6

  1. Which local brand would you be saddest to see bought by a global company? Why that one?
  2. Can a small company keep its personality after joining a giant? What would it need?
  3. On the first day after a takeover, staff are nervous and rumours spread. What would you tell them, and what would you avoid promising?
  4. Should employees get a share of the money when their company is sold? Why, and how much would be fair?

Docket 7

  1. Have you ever been part of a group that joined with another, such as two clubs, classes or teams? What went well or badly?
  2. Who usually loses most in a takeover: the staff, the customers or the founders? Explain your choice.
  3. A big group buys your favourite independent brand and promises 'nothing will change'. What would you watch for to see if that's true?
  4. Should big takeovers have to be approved by staff or customers, not just shareholders? What would that change?

Docket 8

  1. What is the biggest change you've noticed after a shop or brand you use changed owners: prices, quality or service?
  2. Can you trust a promise made during a takeover? What would make it believable?
  3. You negotiate for a small company that is being bought. You can ask for just one guarantee in the contract. Which would it be, and why?
  4. Do mergers make markets better for customers, or do they simply reduce competition? Give an example.
