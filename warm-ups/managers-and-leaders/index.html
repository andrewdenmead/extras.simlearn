Warm-up · EIM · C1

Managers and leaders

Some people manage, some people lead, and the best can do both. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Think of the best manager you've had in a job or internship. What exactly did they do that made you want to work hard?
  2. Some people say leaders are made, not born. Which quality of a good leader do you think is hardest to learn, and why?
  3. Would you rather work for a boss who is brilliant but cold, or one who is warm but often disorganised? Why?
  4. Charismatic leaders inspire people but can make teams depend on them. What happens to a company when a star leader suddenly leaves?

Docket 2

  1. Have you led a group project at university? What was the hardest part of being the one in charge?
  2. Do managers need to be liked by their team to be effective? What changes when they're respected but not liked?
  3. You're promoted to lead your former classmates or friends. Would you keep the friendships close, or create some distance? What does each cost?
  4. Young founders often lead people older and more experienced than themselves. What problems can that create, and how could a young leader earn authority?

Docket 3

  1. Think of a time someone gave you an instruction with no explanation. How did it affect how well you did the job?
  2. Which is more important in a manager: knowing the answers or asking good questions? What would someone who disagrees say?
  3. Would you prefer a boss who checks in on you every day, or one you hardly see until the deadline? Who suits each style?
  4. Many start-ups grow from ten to two hundred people in a few years. What should the founders stop doing themselves, and why is that so hard?

Docket 4

  1. Name a public figure in business, sport or politics whose leadership you admire. What would their critics say about them?
  2. Which tells you more about a leader: how they behave on a good day, or how they behave when things go wrong? Give an example.
  3. Would you rather work for a manager who sets a bold vision and leaves the details to you, or one who gives you a detailed plan? Why?
  4. Sometimes a leader has to choose between being kind to one person and being fair to the whole team. Describe a situation like that. What would you do?

Docket 5

  1. What's one habit of a bad manager that you've experienced, or heard about from friends with part-time jobs?
  2. What happens when a manager admits they don't know the answer? How might the team react, and how might the manager's own boss react?
  3. Gut feeling or hard data: which would you trust more from a leader making a big decision under pressure? Why?
  4. Leaders of famous companies are often celebrated as geniuses. How much of the credit really belongs to them, and how much to the people around them?

Docket 6

  1. Think of a job, club or team where you felt your opinion genuinely mattered. What did the person in charge do to create that feeling?
  2. A manager is popular with the team but often slow to make decisions. What does that cost, and who notices first?
  3. Would you rather your manager were an expert in your field, or a skilled people manager who knows little about the work itself? Why?
  4. Many people leave a job because of their manager, not the company. How far should companies judge managers on whether their staff stay?

Docket 7

  1. Think of a time you had to take charge without officially being in charge. What happened, and how did people react?
  2. How much should a manager share about the company's problems with the team: everything, or only what people need to know? Who benefits from each?
  3. Would you rather earn more in a team with a difficult boss, or less with a boss you really respect? How much more would it take?
  4. In some cultures bosses are expected to be clearly in charge; in others, to be one of the team. Which have you experienced, and which works better internationally?

Docket 8

  1. Have you ever worked or studied under someone who micromanaged? How did it change the way you worked?
  2. Is a good manager automatically a good leader? Think of someone who was one but not the other.
  3. Would you rather have a leader who rarely changes their mind, or one who changes it whenever they hear a good argument? What are the risks of each?
  4. When a company has a scandal, the boss often resigns. Does that actually fix anything, or is it mainly for show? What would someone who disagrees say?
