Warm-up · EBC · C1

Partnerships and co-branding

When two brands make something together, both names are on the line. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk about why brands team up, what makes a partnership feel right, and who takes the risk.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Which brand collaboration have you noticed recently? Did it make you want either brand more?
  2. The BVG and Adidas once made trainers that worked as a yearly travel ticket. Was that a clever partnership or just a gimmick? Who gained most?
  3. Your small design studio gets a collaboration offer from a much bigger brand. What would you check before saying yes?
  4. When two brands team up, which one takes more of the risk: the famous one or the smaller one? Explain.

Docket 2

  1. What is the last thing you bought only because it was a limited collaboration? Do you still use it?
  2. Do collaborations make brands look more creative, or just more desperate for attention? Give an example.
  3. A brand you're working with is suddenly involved in a scandal. Do you end the collaboration, defend them, or wait? Why?
  4. Nike once made trainers with Harris Tweed from the Scottish islands. Who benefits most when a giant works with a small traditional producer?

Docket 3

  1. Which two brands would you love to see make something together? What would it be, and who would buy it?
  2. Does a collaboration with a famous brand make a product better, or just more expensive?
  3. Your brand's best customers are over 50. A partner promises you young buyers. What would you be willing to change, and what never?
  4. Should universities partner with companies on courses and research? What might students gain, and what might they lose?

Docket 4

  1. Think of a collaboration that felt wrong to you. What exactly didn't fit?
  2. Is a co-branded product a sign that a brand is confident, or that it's running out of ideas?
  3. You and a partner disagree about the final design of your joint product, and the deadline is next week. How would you settle it?
  4. When a collaboration becomes a resale hit worth ten times its price, who should profit: the brands, the resellers or the first buyers?

Docket 5

  1. Which brands do you often see together, like a phone and a music app or a car and a coffee chain? Does the link make sense?
  2. What makes a collaboration feel genuine rather than just a marketing stunt? Give an example.
  3. A sportswear brand asks to put your university's name on its clothes. What conditions would you set, as the university?
  4. Do collaborations between luxury and street brands break down class barriers in fashion, or just sell expensive hype to young people?

Docket 6

  1. Would you queue overnight for a limited collaboration? What would it have to be?
  2. Does a partner's reputation become your reputation? Think of a case where one brand damaged the other.
  3. Your partner wants access to all your customer data as part of the deal. What would you share, and what wouldn't you?
  4. Is it fair that small brands often need a big partner to be noticed at all? What does that say about the market?

Docket 7

  1. Which collaboration made you see one of the brands differently? In what way?
  2. Which is the bigger risk in a partnership: choosing the wrong partner or signing the wrong contract? Why?
  3. Your group-project partner from another course has a completely different working style. How would you share the work and the credit?
  4. When a long-established brand works with a young, fashionable one, who benefits more in the long run? Explain.

Docket 8

  1. What is the strangest brand collaboration you've ever seen? Why do you think it happened?
  2. Do collaborations help old brands reach younger people, or do young people see straight through them?
  3. A partner offers you a lot of money but wants to change your logo for the collection. Where would you draw the line?
  4. Which matters more in a collaboration: making money or making something new? What happens when the two clash?
