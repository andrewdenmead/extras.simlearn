Warm-up · EIM · C1

Business ethics

Doing the right thing in business often costs money, time or customers. Pick a docket with your neighbour and talk through all four prompts.

Pick a docket with your neighbours and talk through its four questions. Explain your reasons, and ask what someone who disagrees would say.

All eight dockets

Docket 1

  1. Have you ever stopped buying from a company for ethical reasons? How long did it last, and what brought you back, if anything?
  2. Who carries more responsibility for cheap products made unethically: the company selling them or the customers buying them?
  3. Would you rather work for a company with average pay and an excellent ethical reputation, or one paying 20% more with a doubtful one? Why?
  4. A highly profitable company cuts 2,000 jobs to raise its share price. Is that just business or an ethical failure? What would its shareholders say?

Docket 2

  1. Which unethical products or companies do people complain about but keep using anyway? Why is it so hard to stop?
  2. Is it hypocritical to criticise fast fashion while still sometimes ordering from Shein, Zara or H&M? What would you say to someone who calls you a hypocrite?
  3. Which is worse: treating employees badly, damaging the environment or avoiding tax? Rank them and defend your order.
  4. A company donates millions to charity but avoids paying millions in tax. How should we judge it overall, and who gets to decide?

Docket 3

  1. How much extra would you pay for chocolate, bananas or a T-shirt that was ethically made? Where is your limit?
  2. Many graduates want to work for 'purpose-driven' companies. When a company promises to change the world, how can you tell whether it means it?
  3. A government abuses human rights. Should a profitable company withdraw from that country, or stay and try to improve things from inside? What would each side say?
  4. Should a company be judged on what it does, or on what it says it does? Think of a brand whose claims seem bigger than its actions.

Docket 4

  1. Think of a time a company broke a promise to you: a delivery, a refund, a guarantee. How did it handle it, and did you forgive it?
  2. Is it fair to expect employees to work unpaid overtime for a company they believe in? Where does loyalty end?
  3. Which matters most when you choose a supermarket: price, quality, or how it treats its suppliers? Has that changed since you started paying your own rent?
  4. Big companies are under pressure to take public positions on political issues. When should a company speak up, and when should it stay out of politics?

Docket 5

  1. Which well-known company has the worst ethical reputation, in your opinion? Is that reputation deserved, or exaggerated?
  2. Germans return billions of bottles through Pfand. Do people behave ethically because it's easy and rewarded, or because they believe in it? What does that mean for companies?
  3. Would you rather buy a cheap phone made in poor working conditions, or a much dearer one from a company with a clean record? Be honest!
  4. Some chief executives earn 300 times what an average worker earns. What would be a fair ratio, and who should decide?

Docket 6

  1. What's the most ethical purchase you've made recently, and the least ethical? Be honest!
  2. Does a company's ethics matter more when it's a small, likeable start-up or a global giant? Why might we judge them differently?
  3. Your favourite brand underpaid its factory workers five years ago but has since fixed it. Would you keep buying? What would change your mind?
  4. Can something be completely legal but still unethical? Give an example from business and say who is harmed.

Docket 7

  1. Have you ever paid more for a product because of the story behind it? Looking back, was it worth it?
  2. Is it ethical to sell a product you wouldn't use yourself? Think of jobs where people do this every day.
  3. Would you rather report a colleague's unethical behaviour to your boss, or talk to the colleague first? What could go wrong with each?
  4. Who should set the ethical rules for business: governments through laws, customers through their choices, or companies themselves? Which works best in practice?

Docket 8

  1. What job would you refuse to do, even for a very high salary? Why that one?
  2. Many people say 'business is business'. When is that a fair defence, and when is it just an excuse?
  3. Free trials that are hard to cancel: clever business or a dishonest trick? Which side would you take, and what would the marketing team say?
  4. Supermarkets throw away tonnes of edible food, partly for legal and safety reasons. Who should solve that: the shops, the government or customers?
